# Indus script: an exploratory decoding research project

This public repository presents the **Shukla model**, a developing proposal for interpreting selected Indus signs and sign sequences. It is intended as a workspace for researchers who are interested in this line of inquiry and want to examine, test, refine, or challenge its assumptions.

## Research position

The proposed readings and interpretations in this repository are **working hypotheses**, not established decipherments. They have not been presented here as complete or independently validated research. The Indus script remains undeciphered, and any proposed relationship to Brahmi or other Indian scripts requires careful, evidence-based evaluation. Similarity, a plausible reading, or a program output is not by itself proof of a historical relationship.

The project has a particular interpretive approach; making that approach explicit is part of research transparency, not a claim that it is the only valid one. We welcome researchers who share an interest in this approach, as well as constructive independent criticism and alternative analyses. The goal is to make the reasoning inspectable and improve it through evidence and scholarly discussion—not to seek agreement at the expense of testing.

## What is here

- `Indus script decoder.ipynb` — a small, exploratory implementation of proposed sign mappings and sequence rules. Its output illustrates the current model only; it is not an authoritative translation.
- `indus_seals_database.csv` — a local log template for recording inputs and provisional outputs. Do not treat generated entries as verified readings.

## Using the notebook

Open the notebook in Jupyter or Google Colab and run its cells. It does not require a GitHub token, clone a repository, or push data to GitHub. The CSV log is written to the notebook's local working directory; review it before sharing. Do not place confidential or sensitive information in public contributions.

The notebook is a prototype. Sign identities, sign order, readings, segmentation, language identification, and proposed historical associations all need explicit evidence and independent testing. The existing mapping table reflects the model's current assumptions and should not be mistaken for a consensus sign list or a validated decipherment.

## How to contribute

Useful contributions include:

- documenting the source and identification of each inscription or sign sequence;
- citing published sign lists, corpora, editions, and relevant scholarship;
- separating directly observed data from editorial choices and interpretive claims;
- proposing tests that could distinguish this model from alternatives, including ways it could be falsified;
- reporting counterexamples, uncertain readings, and competing interpretations;
- improving reproducibility, code clarity, and data provenance.

When suggesting a reading, please provide the object or corpus identifier, source/citation, sign sequence and direction as recorded by the source, any segmentation choices, the proposed interpretation, and the reasoning. Mark uncertainty rather than silently resolving it. Please do not describe an interpretation as established unless appropriate evidence and independent validation support that description.

## Research and evidence

This repository is not a substitute for peer-reviewed publication or specialist editions of archaeological material. Bibliographic and object-level citations should be added as claims and data are documented. At present, users should independently verify all readings, historical claims, and data before citing or reusing them. Please open an issue or submit a pull request with specific sources and reproducible reasoning.

## Data and privacy

The CSV is intended as a local research log. The notebook does not upload it automatically. If you choose to commit a dataset, check that its provenance and reuse terms are clear and that it contains no personal or restricted information. Preserve source citations and document any transcription or normalization.
